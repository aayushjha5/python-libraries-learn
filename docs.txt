
Natural Language Processing : It is a part of computer science and artificial intelligence
 that focuses on the interaction between computers and humans through natural language.

Goal here is to enable computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language in a way that is valuable.

This involves several key tasks, including:

    1. Text Preprocessing: Cleaning and preparing text data for analysis, including tokenization, stemming, and lemmatization.
    2. Part-of-Speech Tagging: Identifying the grammatical parts of speech in a sentence, such as nouns, verbs, adjectives, etc.
    3. Named Entity Recognition (NER): Identifying and classifying named entities in text, such as people, organizations, locations, dates, etc.
    4. Sentiment Analysis: Determining the sentiment or emotional tone of a piece of text, such as positive, negative, or neutral.
    5. Machine Translation: Automatically translating text from one language to another.
    6. Text Summarization: Creating a concise summary of a longer piece of text while retaining its key information.
    7. Language Modeling: Predicting the next word or sequence of words in a sentence based on the context.
    8. Speech Recognition: Converting spoken language into written text.
    9. Chatbots and Conversational Agents: Developing systems that can engage in natural language conversations with users.
    10. Information Retrieval: Extracting relevant information from large datasets or documents based on user queries.

NLP Components :
1. NLU : Natural Language Understanding
2. NLG : Natural Language Generation

1. NLU : Natural Language Understanding : It focuses on comprehending and interpreting human language.
    - Mapping input to useful representations
    - Analyzing different aspects of language

2. NLG : Natural Language Generation : It focuses on generating human-like text based on given data or context.
    - Text Planning - includes retrieving relevent content from the knowledge base
    - Sentence Planning - choosing required words from meaningful phrases to set tone of the sentences
    - Text Realization - converting the sentence plan into actual text

NLU Ambiguity Types :
1. Lexical Ambiguity : A word has multiple meanings
    ex - "bank" can mean a financial institution or the side of a river.
2. Syntactic / Structural / Grammatical Ambiguity : A sentence can be interpreted in multiple ways due to its structure
    ex - The chicken is ready to eat.
3. Referential Ambiguity : Uncertainty about what a pronoun or noun phrase refers to
    ex - "John told Mike that he would  go to the store." (Who is "he"? John or Mike?)

Tokenization : process of breaking down text into smaller units called tokens.

Types of Tokenization :


1. Break a complex sentence into words.
2. Understand the importance of each of the words with respect to the sentence.
3. Produce a structural description of an input sentence.


1. Sentence Tokenization : Splitting text into individual sentences. 
2. Word Tokenization : Splitting sentences into individual words or tokens.
3. Subword Tokenization : Breaking down words into smaller meaningful units called subwords.

Frequency Distribution : A statistical representation that shows how often each unique item (word, token, etc.) appears in a given text or corpus.

Advanced Tokenization Techniques :
1. Ngram Tokenization : It involves breaking down text into contiguous sequences of 'n' items (words or characters).
2. Bigram Tokenization : It involves breaking down text into pairs of consecutive words.
3. Trigram Tokenization : It involves breaking down text into triplets of consecutive words.

# Stemming : It is the process of reducing words to their root or base form by removing prefixes and suffixes.
 # not recommended for production use as it can produce non-dictionary words

 # limitations of stemming :
 1. Overstemming : When different words are reduced to the same root form, leading to loss of meaning.
 2. Understemming : When related words are not reduced to the same root form, leading to missed connections.
 3. Irregular Forms : Stemming algorithms may struggle with irregular word forms that do not follow standard patterns.
 4. Context Sensitivity : Stemming does not consider the context in which a word is used, which can lead to incorrect interpretations.

# Lemmatization : It is the process of reducing words to their base or dictionary form, known as a lemma.
 # more accurate than stemming as it considers the context and meaning of the word
 # recommended for production use

 # limitations of lemmatization :
 1. Requires More Resources : Lemmatization typically requires access to a comprehensive dictionary or lexical database, which can be resource-intensive.
 2. Slower Processing : Due to its complexity and reliance on linguistic rules, lemmatization can be slower than stemming, especially for large datasets.
 3. Ambiguity in Context : While lemmatization considers context, it may still struggle with words that have multiple meanings depending on their usage 
    in a sentence.
 4. Language Dependency : Lemmatization algorithms are often language-specific, meaning that a lemmatizer designed for one language may not work effectivel
    for another without significant modifications.

# POS : Part of Speech Tagging : It is the process of assigning grammatical tags to words in a sentence based on their role and function.
 # It helps in understanding the structure and meaning of sentences.


 # POS Tags Examples :
    - Noun (NN): Represents people, places, things, or ideas. Examples: 'dog', 'city', 'happiness'
    - Verb (VB): Represents actions or states of being. Examples: 'run', 'is', 'think'
    - Adjective (JJ): Describes or modifies nouns. Examples: 'happy', 'blue', 'quick'
    - Adverb (RB): Modifies verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. Examples: 'quickly', 'very', 'well'
    - Symbol (SYM): Represents symbols or special characters. Examples: '$', '%', '&'
    - Pronoun (PRP): Replaces nouns to avoid repetition. Examples: 'he', 'she', 'it'
    - Preposition (IN): Shows relationships between nouns and other words. Examples: 'in', 'on', 'at'
    - Conjunction (CC): Connects words, phrases, or clauses. Examples: 'and', 'but', 'or'
    - Interjection (UH): Expresses strong emotions or reactions. Examples: 'oh', 'wow', 'ouch'

# Limitation of POS Tagging :
 1. Ambiguity : Many words can serve multiple grammatical roles depending on the context, leading to potential misclassification.
 2. Context Sensitivity : POS tagging algorithms may struggle to accurately interpret words in complex sentences or idiomatic expressions.
 3. Domain Dependency : POS taggers trained on specific domains may not perform well when applied to texts from different fields or genres.
 4. Incomplete Lexicons : The accuracy of POS tagging can be limited by the comprehensiveness of the underlying lexicon or training data 
    used by the algorithm.
    
# Named Entity Recognition (NER) : It is the process of identifying and classifying named entities in text into predefined categories such 
                                   as persons, organizations, locations, dates, etc.

# Phases of NER:
1. Noun Phrase Identification : Identifying potential named entities in the text,
   typically by looking for capitalized words or specific patterns 
   using POS tagging and dependency parsing.
2. Phrase Classification : Classifying the identified noun phrases into predefined categories 
   (e.g., person, organization, location) using machine learning algorithms or rule-based approaches.
   Apart from this,  one can curate the lookup tables and dictionaries by combining information from
    various sources.
3. Entity Disambiguation : Resolving ambiguities when a named entity can refer to multiple real-world entities.
    Popular Knowledge graphs like Wikidata and DBpedia can be used to link entities to their corresponding entries
    in these databases.

# NER Entities List :
1. PERSON : People, including fictional.
2. FAC : Buildings, airports, highways, bridges, etc.
3. ORG : Organizations, including companies, agencies, institutions, etc.
4. GPE : Countries, cities, states.
5. LOC : Non-GPE locations, mountain ranges, bodies of water.
6. GSPG : Geopolitical entities, including countries, cities, states.
